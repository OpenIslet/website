## Summary

Type 1 diabetes destroys the beta cells that make insulin. Lab-grown islet cells can replace them, but the immune system rejects them unless patients take immune-suppressing drugs for life. Islet-1 is an open research design for stem-cell-derived islets, combining proposed immune-protection edits, an oxygen-supporting setting and a safety switch. Whether the combined product can avoid immune attack and function safely remains unmeasured.

The blueprint compares **Design A**, a four-locus reference family with two cassette layouts, and **Design B**, a family of HLA-C-retaining hypotheses. B1 has six disruption/insertion loci plus an INS edit; B2 has five loci with an optional TYK2 prime edit. Their proposed 2A-free donor cassette is not materialized. The current DNA compiler supports the declared A reference variants, not a complete manufactured B product. **Design X** is the challenger: Design A plus one base edit in the insulin gene ({{editsX}} gene edits and {{baseX}} base edit), with further upgrades that are only proposals. Using {{studies}} study records, {{findings}} extracted findings and {{rules}} Idea Graveyard rules, the model scored {{designs}} combinations. {{blocked}} were blocked by those rules and {{feasible}} remained; the A comparator ranks first in that model, and the X grid challenger second, tied with one other design.

The scores are model estimates, not predictions. {{assumptions}}% of the spread in scores comes from the team's starting assumptions and only {{findingsShare}}% from published findings. **Nothing in the blueprint has been tested in the lab yet.**

## 1. Background and aim

In type 1 diabetes the immune system destroys the beta cells in the pancreatic islets that make insulin. Replacing them is now possible: stem cells can be turned into islet cells, and early reports show insulin production restored in people ([Wang 2024](#ref-39326417); [Carlsson 2025](#ref-40757665)). The unsolved problem is rejection. Cells from a donor are attacked as foreign, so most of today's treatments either need lifelong immune-suppressing drugs, or wall the cells off inside a device, where scar tissue grows and the cells run out of oxygen.

Islet-1 aims for a third route: a personalised therapy whose cells are edited intended to reduce immune attack, with proposed inflammation-responsive protection, an oxygen source for the early graft period, and a safety switch that must be qualified across every graft lineage. The furthest human evidence for the idea is one recipient whose gene-edited donor islets (not stem-cell-derived) survived without immunosuppression ([Carlsson 2025](#ref-40757665)). No multi-edit, stem-cell-derived islet has yet completed a multi-patient trial without drugs.

## 2. The five tests

> **In short.** A graft fails if any one of five tests fails, so a design has to pass all of them.

| Test | The question | How the design responds | Main genes |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **1. Insulin response** | Do the cells release the right amount of insulin when sugar rises, and stop within about 3 minutes when it falls? | A mature mix of 60% beta, 30% alpha and 10% delta cells, grown in clusters of 120 µm or less (80–100 µm in ordered Stage-0 microwells) | |
| **2A. Rejection of donor cells** | Can the cells avoid being recognised as foreign by T cells and antibodies? | Switch off the "ID flags" (HLA) that T cells use | *B2M*, *CIITA* |
| **2B. Autoimmune attack** | Can the cells survive the attack that caused the diabetes, including restarts from graft debris? | Small clusters and a pre-built blood supply shed less debris. Protection switches on with inflammation | *CXCL10* locus |
| **3. Early immune attack** | In the first hours, are the cells protected from natural killer cells, macrophages and blood clotting? | HLA-E and CD47 are intended shields; resting HLA-E in Arm 1B is below the model's assumed threshold and protection must be measured | *B2M* locus |
| **4. Cell stress** | Can the cells survive inflammation, the killer-cell toxin granzyme B, insulin-making stress and low oxygen? | SERPINB9 (blocks granzyme B) and PD-L1 ("back off" to T cells), switched on by inflammation | *CXCL10* locus |
| **5. Oxygen and safety** | Do the cells get oxygen for the first 3 days, and can they be removed on command? | Wet-sieved oxygen beads with catalase barrier films (or a 140 µm bead-free sheet comparator), pre-built vessels, and an iCasp9 safety switch | *AAVS1* locus |

*Table 1. The five tests. Test 2 has two parts, so the scoring model has six factors.*

## 4. The edits, one by one

<a id="spec-edits"></a>

<a id="spec-b2m"></a>

### 4.1 *B2M*: ID flags off, two "don't attack me" signals on

The edit places two genes inside the *B2M* gene. Removing *B2M*'s own output switches off HLA class I, the flags that T cells use to recognise foreign cells ([Gornalusse 2017](#ref-28504668)). The two inserted genes are **HLA-E**, which calms natural killer cells ([Gornalusse 2017](#ref-28504668)), and **CD47**, which tells macrophages not to eat the cell ([Hu 2024](#ref-37156915)).

CD47 is inserted in a form anchored to the cell surface. Full-length CD47 sends signals inside the cell that can blunt insulin release ([Ghimire 2023](#ref-37820008)) and block new blood vessels, while the surface-anchored form separated macrophage protection from cell death and blocked vessel growth in non-islet immune, blood-stem-cell and endothelial models ([Xu 2024](#ref-38508139)); binding, membrane processing, phagocytosis and insulin secretion in stem-cell islets remain to be measured.

Two layouts are being compared. In the first, the two genes are read as one unit, joined by a short "2A" sequence. At 4,880 base pairs its repair template is too large for one AAV6 delivery virus, so it would need non-viral delivery. In the second, each gene has its own promoter and they face opposite directions, so there is no 2A joint and the template (3,974 base pairs) fits in one AAV6 virus. CD47 density in resting and inflammatory conditions is an assay endpoint; the 180,000 to 220,000 molecules-per-cell transition in [Hu 2024](#ref-37156915) was measured on virus-titrated primary human islets with clone CC2C6 and Quantibrite beads (while their engineered stem-cell islets exceeded 600,000), so a protective threshold has not been established for this product. In the current Arm 1B donor, an internal EEF1A1 promoter drives CD47 constitutively and an internal 5xISRE promoter drives HLA-E with inflammatory stimulation. Endogenous B2M read-through is not credited as productive HLA-E expression. The surface densities are unvalidated priors, and CD47 is not demonstrated to compensate for low resting HLA-E (rule GY-009). On soluble HLA-E\*01:03, none of the six functional human leader peptides in [Lin 2023](#ref-37264229) reaches a fourfold activating-over-calming receptor gap on its own (kept separate from older HLA-E\*01:01 monomer measurements such as [Valés-Gómez 1999](#ref-10428963)), while VMAPRTLFL acts as a strong activating NKG2C control ([Lauterbach 2015](#ref-26382247)). Stage-0 lab checks therefore test a 20-construct, two-strand splice-cleaned HLA-E comparator panel alongside the untouched reference sequence—comparing candidate leader peptides, linker lengths, an optional CD8-blocking D227K/T228A control, and the silent *HLA-E* stop-signal adjustment against stray splicing into leftover *B2M* exon 2—across paired resting and interferon-stimulated natural killer subsets, viral-reactive killer T cells, regulatory CD8+ T cells, promoter DNA methylation and nearby host genes. For educated `NKG2A− KIR+` and `IL-12/IL-18 SIRPα-low` natural killer cells (`upper95 <= 8%` and `<= 10%`), fallback routing splits *HLA-C* single-chain trimers (`B′-SCT`, `n >= 6` KIR-typed donors) from held *HLA-G1* (`LILRB1`, PDB `6AEE` engineered `C42S/L110I/Q115R` crystal; held pending product registration and gated at uniform `upper95 <= 8%` on sorted `LILRB1− NKG2A− KIR+` cells), and tests `TIGIT-low CD226-high` natural killer cells on `B2M−/−` stem-cell islets for possible *PVR* (`CD155 Ala67Thr`, `rs1058402`, PDB `3UDW`/`6O3O`/`6ARQ`; [Diong 2023](#ref-37675979); [Matsuo 2022](#ref-35947095)) synaptic sign-inversion relative to MHC-I-intact protection ([Brown 2025](#ref-41202120) vs [Chimienti 2022](#ref-36170817)).

<a id="spec-ciita"></a>

### 4.2 *CIITA*: the second set of flags off

Both copies are switched off, which removes HLA class II, the second set of ID flags ([Parent 2021](#ref-34407395)). Stage-0 RNA checks verify that switching *CIITA* off does not leave unintended splice forms or transcripts. Removing HLA class II does not stop the patient's own immune cells from picking up graft debris and presenting it to T cells, which is why the design also keeps clusters small and adds protection that switches on with inflammation (rule GY-010).

<a id="spec-cxcl10"></a>

### 4.3 *CXCL10*: protection that switches on with inflammation

Two protective genes are inserted at the *CXCL10* gene, so they switch on only when inflammation appears. **SERPINB9** blocks granzyme B, the toxin that killer cells fire. **PD-L1** is a "back off" signal for T cells. SERPINB9 is made first, then PD-L1; Stage-0 lab tests compare the reference `SERPINB9–GSG-P2A–PD-L1` cassette against scar-free (`2A`-free) dual-promoter *SERPINB9* donors alongside the reverse gene order while keeping the repair template inside a single AAV6 delivery virus. They also check that nearby inflammatory genes (*CXCL9* and *CXCL11*) and opposite-strand *ART3* transcripts stay undisturbed—comparing one-change-at-a-time silent *PD-L1* variants, a 3′-end cleavage check and an optional single-sided 80-bp boundary element alongside the untouched reference sequence without adding hairpin-forming repeats ([Xie 2017](#ref-28462820))—and test whether PD-L1 shed into cell-free liquid around the cells ([Hira-Miyazawa 2018](#ref-29345283)) can calm a separate immune response on its own. Screening 112 germline type 1 diabetes genetic regions from the top 200 OpenTargets entries ([Robertson 2021](#ref-34127860); [Chiou 2021](#ref-34012112)) after stripping drug-target, MODY, type 2 diabetes and exocrine confounders converged on shared host-immune `IL-1β` and `FasL` bystander killing (rule GY-010) while ruling out 16 misleading gene targets that must not be switched off—including the `16p13` nearest-gene mitophagy regulator *CLEC16A* ([Soleimanpour 2014](#ref-24949970), where intronic risk variants loop instead to inflammatory *DEXI*, [Dos Santos 2019](#ref-30478640)), protective beta-cell survival and lineage brakes (*PTPN2*, [Moore 2009](#ref-19336676); *TNFAIP3*, [Grey 1999](#ref-10523611); *BACH2*, [Marroquí 2014](#ref-24608439); *CTSH*, [Fløyel 2014](#ref-24982147); *SOCS1*; *GLIS3*; *DLK1*; and *INS*), necroptosis-restraining *CASP8* and *FADD* ([Kaiser 2011](#ref-21368762)), and *CXCL10* switch regulators (*JAK1*, *STAT1*, *IFNGR1*, *IRF1* and constrained *TNFRSF1A* [`gnomAD v4 pLI = 0.9999`], alongside lineage asymmetry between beta cells that resist canonical `RIPK1/RIPK3/MLKL` necroptosis in mouse and human `EndoC-βH1` models, [Zhao 2015](#ref-25301392); [Takiishi 2023](#ref-36707047); [Veli 2024](#ref-39004142), versus susceptible blood-vessel endothelium and product-specific efferocytosis checks on adhesion-knockout proposals, [Hammer 2024](#ref-38981470); [Saha 2025](#ref-40796566), plus unresolved pleiotropic `12q13.2` *ERBB3*, [Kaur 2016](#ref-26450151)). Alongside the untouched reference sequence, Stage-0 lab tests therefore evaluate a held six-arm synthetic-intron challenger panel (`Multi-miR(IL1R1 + FAS)`, 4,398-bp donor / 4,688 bp inside the 4,700-bp single-AAV6 cap, with scramble and single-hairpin factorial controls and zero added foreign peptide stretches in the primary wild-type *SERPINB9* arm; [Fellmann 2013](#ref-24332856); [Amen 2022](#ref-35880017)) and seven standalone zero-bystander *FAS* and *IL1R1* cytosine-base-editor stop comparators, while retiring soluble endothelial *IL1R2* decoys that cannot outcompete beta-cell *IL1RAcP* in *cis* ([Thomas 2012](#ref-22426547)). Any challenger promotion requires calibrated single-molecule splicing clearance, preserved `miR-375` Argonaute-2 occupancy ([Poy 2004](#ref-15538371)), >= 85% retained interferon-driven SERPINB9 and PD-L1 output, unblunted postprandial `IL-1β`-amplified insulin release ([Dror 2017](#ref-28092375)) and >= 99.99% AP1903 `iCasp9` kill.

The design retains STAT1 because its proposed interferon-responsive trigger depends on that pathway. Switching STAT1 off while relying on that trigger is incompatible with the proposed mechanism, so the model blocks it: a design conflict, not a failed experiment (rule GY-001). [Sintov 2022](#ref-36055241) reports that targeting interferon-induced signals can help stem-cell islets survive, so the design has to choose between that and its own interferon trigger. One detail is unresolved: the flexible loop of SERPINB9 that grabs granzyme B cannot be modelled reliably by computer, so whether it works as designed needs a lab test.

<a id="spec-aavs1"></a>

### 4.4 *AAVS1*: the safety switch

A switch gene, iCasp9, is inserted at a "safe harbour" site called *AAVS1*, on both copies of the chromosome ([Wunderlich 2022](#ref-35795779)). AP1903 is intended to trigger cell death through the switch. Its promoter is intended for broad expression, but reliable expression and drug-triggered killing across endocrine, vascular, immature and off-target cells remain lab tests the switch has to pass ([Yagyu 2016](#ref-27626039)). A switch that worked only in mature beta cells would stay silent in immature or rogue cells and could not remove them (rule GY-002). The inserted element is the HNRNPA2B1-proximal 455-bp CpG-core candidate at GRCh38 chr7:26200580-26201034; the legacy CBX3 label is an alias, not proof of identity to the published CBX3-side fragment. Stage-0 lab tests compare four anti-silencing and orientation arms alongside matched two-strand splice-cleaned comparators ([Knight 2012](#ref-22696657)) at *AAVS1*—leaving out intron-blocking stop signals so host *PPP1R12C* transcription can run through—while checking *PPP1R12C* splicing, double-stranded RNA stress, calibrated single-molecule RNA junctions and DNA methylation alongside the untouched reference sequence. Three crystal structures of the switch's separate parts (PDB 1BL4, 1JXQ and 1NW9) check local protein geometry, while whole-switch killing across every lineage remains a lab test. The proposed target is an escape bound of fewer than one in 100 million cells capable of regrowth, not a measured kill rate.

<a id="spec-designb"></a>

### 4.5 What Design B changes

The B family disrupts *HLA-A*, *HLA-B* and *CIITA* while retaining matched *HLA-C* ([Xu 2019](#ref-30853558)). B1 additionally disrupts TYK2 and proposes an INS edit; B2 instead preserves TYK2 or proposes its P1104A prime edit ([Dendrou 2016](#ref-27807284); [Boisson-Dupuis 2018](#ref-30578352)), and in the four-hurdle assessment is paired with Section 5's `140 µm` bead-free pre-vascularized sheet comparator as an independent chemical-free route. The differentiation penalties for switching off TYK2 are literature-anchored priors, not observations of this combined chassis ([Chandra 2022](#ref-36289205)). Because *TYK2* P1104A replaces a proline at position 2 of an internal nine-amino-acid segment with an alanine anchor favoured by *HLA-C* (`SPPTKFLEL` to `SAPTKFLEL`; [Rasmussen 2014](#ref-25311805); [O'Donnell 2020](#ref-32711842); [Finton 2023](#ref-37207206)), and more than 90% of potential recipients carry wild-type *TYK2* on both chromosomes, Stage-0 lab checks enforce a fail-closed hold gate on Design B2 across separate recipient *TYK2* genotype strata (`G/G`, `G/C` and `C/C`) and *HLA-C* alleles: any human CD8+ T-cell killing or priming in islet or blood-vessel cells—or mass-spectrometry detection of `SAPTKFLEL` on *HLA-C*—holds the prime-edited B2 variant even if the other readout looks clean. Each *HLA-C* allele is also tracked by its own SP-1C, SP-2C or SP-3C leader peptide ([Lin 2023](#ref-37264229)), kept separate from the *HLA-B* −21M/−21T leader split ([Ramsuran 2018](#ref-29302013); rule GY-005). The proposed 2A-free protective cassette has no complete donor ledger, though comparing existing 2A-free *CXCL10* test templates against the canonical P2A template across the 22-species foreign-peptide scan trims reference-unmatched 8–11-amino-acid stretches from 205 to 83 in wild-type-*TYK2* Design B (243 to 121 in *TYK2*-P1104A Design B2) while porcine P2A itself remains untested in human T-cell studies ([Arber 2013](#ref-23698740)). Existing Layer 2 B profiles are explicitly provisional P2A experimental comparators and cannot establish an exact B product's benefit. HLA-C-mediated protection, editing safety and manufacturing performance all remain unmeasured. Cutting *HLA-A* and *HLA-B* at the same time risks deleting or flipping the stretch of DNA between them (about 1.4 million letters), which holds *HLA-E* and the *HLA-C* copy Design B keeps. Design B therefore has to make the two edits one after the other, checking the cells in between, or use editors that do not cut both strands of the DNA.

<a id="spec-designx"></a>

### 4.6 What Design X changes

Design X keeps Design A's four edits and adds one: a base edit in the insulin gene (*INS*). A base edit changes a single letter of DNA without cutting both strands. This one alters the insulin signal sequence and silently removes a hidden start signal that can make a faulty insulin-like fragment, which T cells in people with type 1 diabetes have been reported to attack ([Kracht 2017](#ref-28263308)). The aim is to leave the graft's own insulin a harder target for the autoimmune attack (test 2B). It cannot hide the other proteins the attack targets (GAD65, IA-2 and ZnT8), or stop the patient's own immune cells passing on cell debris, so rule GY-010 stays on as a warning. Whether the edit helps, and whether insulin still works normally after it, is unmeasured.

The name Design X covers two separate ideas. The one above, with its insulin-gene edit, is the one the fixed-grid computer model scores. Separately, Stage-0 upgrades are proposed under the same name (and evaluated in the four-hurdle assessment). They are not part of the fixed-grid score, none has been tested in the lab yet, and they do not include the insulin-gene edit.

- **Redesigned "don't attack me" and bystander-receptor shields.** At the *B2M* locus, a new DNA switch, a cysteine-cleaned CD47 protein and a 20-construct splice-cleaned *HLA-E* comparator panel ([Lin 2023](#ref-37264229); [Lauterbach 2015](#ref-26382247)) test higher resting HLA-E while avoiding activating NKG2C natural killer cells (alongside an optional `D227K/T228A` control against viral-reactive killer T cells). At the *CXCL10* locus, a six-arm synthetic-intron `Multi-miR(IL1R1 + FAS)` shield ([Fellmann 2013](#ref-24332856); [Amen 2022](#ref-35880017)) dials down the cell's own IL-1 and Fas death receptors inside a single AAV6 virus.
- **A protective stem-cell line and base-editor comparators.** A starting line that already carries a natural variant of the insulin gene linked to a lower risk of type 1 diabetes ([van Tienhoven 2025](#ref-40112799)), alongside testing how a naturally protective *TYK2* variant ([Dendrou 2016](#ref-27807284)) behaves across the {{virtualPatients}}-patient simulation and comparing seven standalone zero-bystander *FAS* and *IL1R1* cytosine-base-editor stop alleles. Choosing a starting line is a selection step, not an edit. In the one mouse comparison reported for the insulin variant, it did not clearly change how many mice were cured, so any protection is unproven.
- **Ordered microwells, recombinant human catalase barrier films and an outer-ring gel.** Pairs ordered microwells (capping clusters at 80–100 µm against 3D crowding, [Balboa 2022](#ref-35241836)), wet-sieved calcium-peroxide beads (`d_99 <= 50 µm`) and recombinant human catalase (`P04040`) barrier films at least 20 µm thick around a thinner 100 µm central oxygen layer, while placing clotting-control (SA-THBD, [Turan 2024](#ref-38709159)) and Fas-ligand (SA-FasL, [Headen 2018](#ref-29867165); [Lei 2022](#ref-35559682)) gel particles strictly in an outer boundary ring away from the beta cells. Because bacterial streptavidin is completely foreign to the human proteome (baseline human reactivity in [Berth 2018](#ref-29466233); [Verougstraete 2020](#ref-31782946), and clinical secondary antibody surges after prior exposure in [Breitz 2000](#ref-10647616); [Orozco 2025](#ref-40605797)) and many people carry pre-existing anti-PEG antibodies ([Yang 2016](#ref-27804292)), Stage-0 lab tests challenge the retained `SA-FasL` / `SA-THBD` cuff across unpooled human sera stratified by baseline anti-PEG and Day-28 anti-streptavidin IgG alongside a held streptavidin-free human comparator panel (`CFH[19–448]` + `THBD[363–497]-Cys` + `ADIPOQ-Hexa-FasL`, [Verbrugge 2009](#ref-19276268); [Eisele 2011](#ref-21183510); [Jandu 2020](#ref-32619459)) conjugated via self-hydrolyzing `DPR`-maleimide linkers ([Lyon 2014](#ref-25194818), `>= 98%` ring hydrolysis, `<= 5%` 14-day bioactive FasL shedding, and `72-hour` primary human hepatocyte leachate/digest safety). Stage-0 tests also compare never-treated, gel-depleted and active grafts side by side for immune rebound, and test an optional complement shield (CD55) on the exact same edited cells while keeping the cell's own CD59 insulin-release machinery intact ([Golec 2022](#ref-35666870)).

Across all three designs, the zinc transporter gene *SLC30A8* (ZnT8) is kept intact until stem-cell islet yield and insulin output are measured. Four other proposed edits—thymidylate synthase (*TYMS*) nutrient control ([Sartori-Maldonado 2024](#ref-38867450)), tuning down *NLRC5* in Design B, switching on the cell's own *CD274* (PD-L1) promoter, and tuning down *TXNIP*—are held outside all core designs behind written Stage-0 pass rules.

<a id="spec-environment"></a>

## 5. The environment: oxygen and blood supply

> **In short.** A new graft has no blood vessels at first, so the design brings its own oxygen and vessels.

The cells are grown into clusters of 120 µm or less across, so oxygen can reach the centre. A graft would be a thin slab about 350 µm thick. It holds the clusters, pre-built blood-vessel fragments (the plan is to grow them from the same cell line, since stem cells can form blood vessels, [Wimmer 2019](#ref-30651639); the one measured result so far used fragments taken from fat tissue, [Aghazadeh 2021](#ref-34480863)), and a central layer that releases oxygen for the first 72 hours (calcium peroxide in silicone, with the enzyme catalase turning the by-product hydrogen peroxide into oxygen).

The margin is thin. In the baseline used for the scores, a 350 µm slab with a central layer about a third of its thickness leaves two outer bands of about 115 µm, a little narrower than the largest clusters (120 µm), and with pessimistic assumptions the model's oxygen at the middle of a cluster is low. The version of Design A planned for the Stage-0 lab tests, and the Design X proposals, thin the central layer to 100 µm, which leaves outer bands of 125 µm and caps clusters at 100 µm, packing the same calcium peroxide inventory into 46.39 wt% (23.45 vol%) beads instead of 31.25 wt% (13.86 vol%). Because the model's 68-hour oxygen-release half-life comes from a 25 wt% (10.55 vol%) solid silicone disk ([Pedraza 2012](#ref-22371586)) rather than catalase-coated microbeads ([Accolla 2023](#ref-36971050); [Ke 2022](#ref-35743287)), an 84-run sensitivity sweep tests faster and slower half-lives across multiple release laws and shows that the passing window narrows sharply when blood vessels connect late; each recipe's particle size, release law and half-life have to be measured in its implanted form, and none of this has been measured yet.

The tissue model couples oxygen supply and consumption, peroxide clearance, acid–base balance, cell viability and a proposed vascular contribution over 14 days, including joint sensitivity checks where blood vessels connect late and supply less oxygen alongside a zero-vessel lower bound. Its numerical outputs depend on declared geometry, boundary conditions, reaction parameters and spatial discretization. A slab-average or homogenized oxygen value cannot establish the oxygen level inside every discrete cluster. When resolved in three dimensions across 160 random-sequential-adsorption clusters at 1,500 islet equivalents per cm² (`15 µm` physical collagen capsule, equivalent to `45 µm` chronic hydrogel resistance), unpatterned pouring lets `100 µm` clusters crowd one another so `40–60%` fall below the `15 mmHg` surface-oxygen target (`20%` below the `5 mmHg` core floor), whereas ordered microwells (`198.76 / 213.58 µm` square/hexagonal pitch for `100 µm`, or dose-matched `142.22 / 152.83 µm` pitch for `80 µm` clusters preserving `1,500 IEQ/cm²`, kept distinct from pitch-held `768 IEQ/cm²` dose derating; [Balboa 2022](#ref-35241836)) prevent lateral crowding. In three dimensions, a single uncoated calcium-peroxide bead touching the inner lamina edge (`791.5 mg` total inventory) also creates a `6.9–19.5 µM` local hydrogen-peroxide plume next to beta cells with low endogenous catalase ([Lenzen 1996](#ref-8720919); [Neal 2016](#ref-27729543)); Stage-0 lab tests therefore pair wet-sieved beads (`d_99 <= 50 µm`, noting [Accolla 2023](#ref-36971050) tested an ~`17.5 µm` silicone shell rather than catalase) with `>= 20 µm` cell-free barrier films immobilized strictly with **recombinant human catalase (`UniProt P04040`, `0` foreign `8–11`-mers or `15`-mers in both full-length `527-aa` and Met-cleaved `526-aa` forms across `20,429` human Swiss-Prot proteins, disqualifying bovine `P00432` and fungal *Aspergillus niger* `P55303` xeno-catalases and enforcing `<= 0.10 EU/mg` / `<= 0.50 EU` total-implant endotoxin and `<= 30%` 7-day shed-protein mass)** (`~105,429 IU` post-cure active load on `333.3 cm²` to retain `73,800 IU` at `>= 70%` 7-day washout retention and hold `24-hour` burst peroxide to `1.13 µM <= 2.5 µM`, since the zero-added-enzyme `37,800 IU` split reaches `2.82 µM`), qualifying either an expanded `390 µm` slab (`2 × 125 µm` outer bands) or `2 × 105 µm` outer bands inside `350 µm` across the full 14-day transport gates. Any bead-free `140 µm` pre-vascularized comparator ([Pepper 2015](#ref-25893782); [Chendke 2023](#ref-37476069); [Einstein 2023](#ref-38993891)) must pass both five-site 14-day empty-lumen oxygen micro-optode checks (`>= 40 mmHg` within `<= 15 µm` for plain hydrogel, or `>= 35 mmHg` within `<= 20 µm` if hydrated breathable dividers clear materials checks, [Markov 2014](#ref-24065585)) and loaded closed-state confirmation (`>= 15 mmHg` surface, `>= 5 mmHg` core, pimonidazole paired with injury markers per [Olsson 2011](#ref-21788581), and perifusion insulin stimulation index `>= 3.0`), since `25 mmHg` plain hydrogel fails across acute rest, chronic `1.097×` demand (`D_gel = 1.5 × 10^-5 cm²/s`, `k_perf = 0.1358 s^-1` vs acute `9.40 × 10^-6 cm²/s`, `0.097 s^-1`) and passive `5 vol%` perfluorocarbon emulsion (`11.4–12.4 mmHg < 15 mmHg`). Baseline `791.5 mg` calcium peroxide and `36/60-hour` vascular timing bounds ([Van Simaeys 2026](#ref-42547502)) are kept unchanged: Layer 4 separates whole-slab `(m_endo + 0.15 × m_vasc) / 1.15` demand from pure-endocrine single-cluster oxygen consumption (`14.3%` vascular cells vs `13.04%` modeled vascular demand; post-washout vs acute rodent-islet respiration and human alpha-cell `K_ATP` sensitivity in [Ma 2013](#ref-23935835); [Sweet 2006](#ref-17130499); [MacDonald 2007](#ref-17503968)), marine annelid hemoglobin `M101` (`5 g/L` in a closed `10 mL` slab = `2.17 µmol` total vs `5,491 µmol` from `CaO2`) unloads oxygen above `15 mmHg` for `< 9 seconds` as a standalone finite reservoir ([Rousselot 2006](#ref-16897713); distinct from air-exposed culture in [Lemaire 2023](#ref-37318185)), unencapsulated channel blockers wash out of `140–350 µm` slabs in `7–44 seconds` (`28–177 seconds` one-sided, `D = 2.8 × 10^-6 cm²/s`), and Stage-0 checks require both a `120-hour` assembled-organoid respirometry coupon (`upper95 <= 0.80` with encapsulated depot `t_1/2 >= 72 h` and post-washout `GSIS SI >= 3.0` / `>= 85%` output / glucagon fold `>= 2.0` before any coupled Stage-2 `CaO2` reduction test) and a four-arm `24-hour` pre-encapsulation `Nec-1s` (`10 µM`) × `Ferrostatin-1` (`2 µM`) conditioning check (holding `100 µM` benchmark and `Nec-1` anti-ferroptotic comparators, [Aggarwal 2026](#ref-41951603); [Takahashi 2012](#ref-23190609); [Yuk 2021](#ref-34572979); [Bruni 2018](#ref-29789532); [Li 2020](#ref-32893325)) gated on `< 10 nM` final-wash supernatant, washed cell-pellet `LC-MS <= LOQ` (`>= 80%` recovery), `>= 90%` undiluted endocrine and vascular `NQO1` activity ([Yu 2021](#ref-34252539)), `>= 85%` viable `SC-beta` and `PLVAP+ iEC` yields, and `GSIS SI >= 3.0`. Surviving the 14-day low-oxygen window also does not guarantee normal hormone control afterwards ([Wang 2025](#ref-40404627)), so Stage-0 lab tests measure insulin and glucagon release during and after recovery before counting the working dose. Current values belong to the versioned tissue atlas and must be read with its sensitivity and numerical-convergence checks; none is an experimentally established safety margin.

A separate 24-hour hormone model explores the proposed beta/alpha/delta mixture, meals and exercise, logging conditional portal insulin-washout lag bounds across `150,000` (`v = 0.18`, kept distinct from Stage-5A's `25,000–35,000 IEQ` forearm sentinel), `300,000` (`v = 0.36`, `T_ins <= 3.64 min`) and non-waivable full-dose `500,000` total `IEQ` (`v = 0.60`, `T_ins <= 1.96–2.71 min`). Its glucose nadir and hormone dynamics are conditional scenario outputs; they do not prove protection from hypoglycaemia or validate insulin shut-off in a patient. Alongside pure beta-cell controls ([Perez-Frances 2024](#ref-39169271)), Stage-0 lab tests compare higher-beta cell mixes (70:20:10 to 80:10:10) against the 60:30:10 default, keeping 60:30:10 unless fresh batches confirm both clean insulin shut-off and low-sugar glucagon rescue.

## 6. The evidence behind it

The corpus contains {{studies}} study records, {{arms}} experimental and control groups and {{findings}} extracted findings. Alongside that scoring baseline, the expanded Type 1 Diabetes [Context–Evidence Graph](/ceg) maps {{cegStudies}} primary empirical studies, {{cegArms}} experimental and control groups, {{cegOutcomes}} structured outcomes and {{cegLinks}} blueprint links screened from {{cegScreened}} candidate records; design scores stay on the {{studies}}-study scoring set unless a proposal updates a model parameter or rule. Active empirical studies, superseded reports and other record classes are distinguished in the source data. Evidence records carry source pointers, and retained verbatim excerpts are checked against their source under the public quotation policy. Evidence against an idea is included alongside evidence for it. For example, CD47 protects against macrophages but can blunt insulin release. The blueprint was then checked at five layers: the literature, the DNA ({{constructs}} genomic reference constructs, two-strand RNA-splicing and repeat scans, a human-Swiss-Prot-subtracted synthetic-peptide census across 22 cellular molecular species plus extracellular biomaterial/cuff proteins, and pre-registered Stage-0 donor comparators; AlphaGenome scores are neither published nor used as evidence, because its terms limit outputs to non-commercial use), the proteins ({{structures}} pinned 3D reference structures—13 experimental PDBs across the core edit targets plus 2 AlphaFold models—covering {{proteinPairs}} protein pairs, supplemented by a 13-structure peptide-bound HLA-E crystal survey excluding unliganded 3BDW, four cytokine/death-receptor crystal checks, four PVR and HLA-G1 receptor crystal coordinate audits (`3UDW`, `6O3O`, `6ARQ`, `6AEE`), biomaterial catalase/cuff structural and bioconjugation audits, soluble receptor-affinity reconciliations and non-gating CC-BY-4.0 AlphaMissense v3 variant provenance, [Cheng 2023](#ref-37733863)), tissue and body simulations, and a scenario program using {{virtualPatients}} synthetic population members. Sequence, peptide-census, two-strand splice-motif and DNA-grammar checks flag places for the lab to test (reconciling the 326 Class-I and 112 Class-II boundary peptide windows into 285 and 105 reference-unmatched stretches alongside internal 2A, precursor, readthrough and point-edit stretches); they cannot certify that an insertion leaves nearby genes, splicing, immune tolerance or clonal safety undisturbed, so each core edit site carries calibrated single-molecule RNA, DNA-methylation and deep duplex-sequencing checks in Stage 0 while reference sequences stay untouched.

## 7. How the designs are scored

Each design gets a score from 0 to 1 on each test. Because the tests run in series, the scores are multiplied. A penalty applies for each gene edit beyond four and for each base edit. Designs that contain a known dead end score zero. {{designs}} designs were scored: {{blocked}} scored zero and {{feasible}} remained.

<!--figure:scores-->

**These scores are not predictions.** {{assumptions}}% of the spread comes from starting assumptions built into the model, {{overlap}}% from the overlap between assumptions and findings, and only {{findingsShare}}% from published findings. They are a structured way to compare designs and to see where the weak points are, not a chance of success. Lab results will replace the assumptions.

## 9. Known risks and open questions

- **Hour-zero levels.** CD47 and HLA-E may be too low in the first hours, when natural killer cells and macrophages attack.
- **SERPINB9.** Whether its flexible loop blocks granzyme B as designed cannot be settled by computer.
- **Indirect attack.** Cells without HLA flags can still be attacked when the patient's own immune cells present their debris.
- **One *HLA-C* gene** may not calm natural killer cells (Design B).
- **Editing *HLA-A* and *HLA-B* together** (Design B) can delete or flip the DNA between them, including *HLA-E* and the *HLA-C* copy it keeps, so the edits have to be made one after the other.
- **An insulin-only edit** cannot hide the other targets of the autoimmune attack (Design X).
- **Thin oxygen margin, cluster crowding, local peroxide and pre-conditioning washout.** Margins tighten under pessimistic oxygen-release laws, late blood vessels, unpatterned cluster crowding or edge-touching peroxide beads; baseline `791.5 mg CaO2` is never derated without `120-hour` simultaneous assembled-organoid respirometry (`<= 0.80×`, [Ma 2013](#ref-23935835); [Sweet 2006](#ref-17130499); [MacDonald 2007](#ref-17503968); standalone closed-slab `M101` bounded `< 9 s` above `15 mmHg`, [Rousselot 2006](#ref-16897713); [Lemaire 2023](#ref-37318185)), pre-encapsulation `Nec-1s × Ferrostatin-1` conditioning requires washed cell-pellet `LC-MS <= LOQ` and `>= 90%` `NQO1` activity ([Aggarwal 2026](#ref-41951603); [Yu 2021](#ref-34252539)), and surviving low oxygen does not guarantee normal insulin and glucagon control afterwards ([Wang 2025](#ref-40404627); [Einstein 2023](#ref-38993891)).
- **Temporary gel signals, biomaterial antibodies and xeno-enzymes.** Once clotting-control and Fas-ligand gel particles wash out over days, the immune system could rebound against the bare edited cells; pre-existing human anti-PEG antibodies ([Yang 2016](#ref-27804292)), secondary anti-streptavidin antibodies on staged dosing ([Breitz 2000](#ref-10647616); [Orozco 2025](#ref-40605797)), unhydrolyzed maleimide retro-Michael shedding ([Lyon 2014](#ref-25194818); [Verbrugge 2009](#ref-19276268)), or commercial bovine/fungal xeno-catalases must all be ruled out by human `P04040` catalase locks and primed-serum / hepatocyte gates.
- **Insertion-site neighbours, two-way splicing and silencing.** Adding genes at *B2M*, *CXCL10* and *AAVS1* or switching off *CIITA* could disturb nearby genes, splice in either direction into host exons (such as *B2M* exon 2, *ART3* or *PPP1R12C*), form repeated hairpins in AAV6 templates, or lose promoter activity over time unless ruled out with calibrated RNA and methylation checks alongside one-change-at-a-time Stage-0 comparators.
- **Cross-talk between immune cell types.** Testing T cells, natural killer cells and macrophages in separate wells keeps each edit's readout clear, but could miss ways those cells amplify one another when combined ([Leite 2020](#ref-32668238)).
- **Many edits at once** may cause DNA rearrangements (adenine base editors did in [Wu 2024](#ref-39529170)) and favour cells with cancer-linked changes, so manufacturing needs deep sequencing checks.
- **The scores rest mostly on assumptions.** No multi-edit, stem-cell-derived islet has completed a multi-patient trial without immunosuppression.
